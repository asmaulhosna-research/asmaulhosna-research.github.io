About Me

My academic interests lie at the intersection of epidemiology, biostatistics, and data science. I am particularly interested in applying rigorous statistical and machine learning methods to important public health questions.

My research interests include cerebrovascular disease, stroke-related outcomes, mental health, cognitive aging, and population health disparities.

Current and Proposed Research Directions

Predicting Life After Stroke

My proposed research focuses on developing and validating statistical and machine learning approaches to study stroke risk, post-stroke depression, and cognitive decline among older adults.

Planned methods include longitudinal modeling, competing-risk analysis, predictive model calibration, fairness assessment, and reproducible R and Python tools.

Status: Proposed research program.
